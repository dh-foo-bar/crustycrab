## Ingredients

- 2 lb Yukon gold potatoes, in ¾ in dice, peel on or off
- 2 qt water
- 2 tbsp kosher salt, for the water
- ½ tsp baking soda
- 3 tbsp fat: olive oil, bacon fat or duck fat
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- ½ tsp garlic powder
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 1 tbsp butter, to finish
- Chives or scallions, flaky salt and hot sauce, to serve

## Steps

1. **Heat the pan.** Put a rimmed sheet pan or 12 in cast iron skillet in the oven and heat to 450°F.
2. **Parboil.** Bring the water, salt and baking soda to a boil. Add the potatoes and cook until a knife slides in with slight resistance and the edges look fuzzy, 8–10 minutes.
3. **Dry.** Drain and let the potatoes steam off in the colander for 2 minutes.
4. **Rough up.** Return them to the pot with the fat, paprika, garlic powder and pepper and toss hard for 30 seconds, until a starchy paste coats every piece. That paste becomes the crust.
5. **Roast.** Spread the potatoes on the hot pan in one layer with space between the pieces. Roast 20 minutes without touching.
6. **Flip.** Release them with a thin metal spatula, turn, and roast 15–25 minutes more, until deep golden all over.
7. **Finish.** Toss with the butter and chives, taste, then add flaky salt. Serve with hot sauce.

## Notes

- **Why baking soda:** alkaline water breaks down the potato surface, releasing starch that fries into a thick crust. Don't use more than ½ tsp or it tastes soapy.
- **Make ahead:** parboil the night before, spread on a tray and chill uncovered. The surface dries and crisps even harder. Roast straight from the fridge, adding about 5 minutes.
- **Onions and peppers** burn at this heat over this long; sauté them separately and fold in at the end, or add them at the flip.
- **Russets** give a crunchier shell but break up more; cut them 1 in and pull them from the water a minute early.
- **Don't crowd the pan:** touching pieces steam. Use two pans for a double batch.
- **At high altitude (around 5,000 ft):** water boils near 202°F, so the parboil takes 10–12 minutes. Go by the knife test, not the clock.
