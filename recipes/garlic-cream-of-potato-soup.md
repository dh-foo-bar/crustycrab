## Ingredients

### Roasted garlic

- 4 whole heads garlic
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- Pinch of salt

### Soup

- 4 tbsp butter
- 2 large onions or leeks, diced
- About 4 lb Yukon gold potatoes, in 1½ in dice, skins on
- 2 small heads cauliflower (8–10 cups florets)
- 8–10 cups vegetable or chicken stock, warm
- 2 tsp dried thyme, or several fresh sprigs
- 1 tsp white pepper
- About ½ cup heavy cream
- 1½ cups grated sharp cheddar (optional)
- Kosher salt

### To finish

- Chives or green onion, extra cheddar, cracked pepper and hot sauce

## Steps

1. **Roast the garlic.** Heat the oven to 400°F. Slice ¼ in off the tops of the garlic heads to expose the cloves, set them on foil, drizzle with the oil and a pinch of salt, wrap, and roast 35–45 minutes, until soft and golden. Squeeze out the cloves once cool enough to handle.
2. **Sweat the onions.** Melt the butter in a 6–8 qt pot over medium-low and cook the onions until soft and translucent, 8–10 minutes, without browning.
3. **Simmer.** Add the potatoes, cauliflower, warm stock, thyme and white pepper. Simmer until both are fork-tender, about 25 minutes at this volume.
4. **Reserve the chunks.** Scoop out about half the potato, roughly 2 lb, and set it aside; these stay whole for texture.
5. **Blend the base.** Squeeze in the roasted garlic and blend the rest of the pot smooth with an immersion blender, working all around the pot.
6. **Bring it together.** Stir the reserved potato back in, then the cream and the cheddar, if using, until melted. Season hard with salt; a pot this size needs more than you'd think, so taste and keep going.
7. **Serve.** Ladle into bowls and top with chives, extra cheddar, cracked pepper and hot sauce.

## Notes

- **Cauliflower as the cream:** simmered and blended, it turns neutral and silky and gives the soup a heavy-cream body without turning gluey the way over-blended potato does. Don't roast it; roasted cauliflower blends grainy.
- **Yukon golds** are buttery and lower in starch, so the chunks hold their shape and the thin skins can stay on.
- **Lighter or richer:** skip the cheese and lean on the cauliflower, or add a little more cheddar.
- **At high altitude (around 5,000 ft):** the vegetables take a little longer to turn fork-tender and the pot loses more liquid, so keep extra warm stock on hand and go by feel, not the clock.
