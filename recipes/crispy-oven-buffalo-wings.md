## Ingredients

- 2 lb chicken wings, split into flats and drumettes
- 1 tbsp aluminum-free baking powder
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ cup cayenne pepper hot sauce
- 4 tbsp unsalted butter
- 1 tbsp honey

## Steps

1. **Dry and season.** Pat the wings completely dry; this is the step that makes or breaks the skin. Toss with the baking powder and salt until evenly coated. Arrange in a single layer on a wire rack set over a sheet pan and rest uncovered in the fridge at least 1 hour, ideally overnight.
2. **Render the fat.** Heat the oven to 250°F with a rack in the lower-middle. Bake the wings about 30 minutes. Nothing will look crisp yet; this phase gently melts the fat out of the skin so it can crisp later instead of drying into leather.
3. **Crisp.** Raise the oven to 450°F and move the pan to the upper-middle. Bake 40–45 minutes, flipping once halfway, until deep golden and crisp. Go by color, not time.
4. **Make the sauce.** Melt the butter in a small pan over low heat, then whisk in the hot sauce and honey until glossy. Keep warm.
5. **Toss and serve.** Rest the wings 2 minutes, then toss them in the warm sauce right before serving so the crust survives.

## Notes

- **Why it works:** the air-drying pulls moisture from the skin, and the baking powder raises its pH so it blisters and browns instead of toughening. Holding one moderate temperature the whole time is what makes leathery wings. Use baking powder, not baking soda, which tastes metallic.
- **Keep them on the rack** the whole time so air circulates underneath; wings lying on the pan steam and turn tough on the bottom.
- **At high altitude (around 5,000 ft):** oven temperatures stay the same, but the dry air dries the skin faster, so the overnight rest is even more effective. If the inside lags, give the hot phase an extra 5 minutes.
- **Sauce variations:** peri-peri sauce with a squeeze of lime, or sriracha with honey and a little soy for a sticky glaze. For dry-rub wings, skip the sauce and season them straight out of the oven.
