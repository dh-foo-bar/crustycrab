## Ingredients

Makes two 9x13 pans. Each pan uses half of everything.

- 6 lb (2.7 kg) Yukon gold potatoes, peeled and sliced ⅛ in thick
- 8 tbsp butter, plus more for the pans
- 2 large shallots, minced
- 10 garlic cloves, minced
- 3 tbsp all-purpose flour
- 4 cups whole milk
- 2 cups heavy cream
- 1 cup chicken or vegetable stock
- 4 tsp Dijon mustard
- 4 tsp Diamond kosher salt (2½ tsp Morton)
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 8 thyme sprigs
- 2 bay leaves
- ¼ tsp ground nutmeg
- Chopped chives, to finish

## Steps

1. **Prep.** Heat the oven to 400°F with racks in the upper and lower thirds, and butter both pans. Slice the potatoes on a mandoline without rinsing them; their starch helps thicken the sauce.
2. **Start the sauce.** For the first pan, melt 3 tbsp of the butter in a large, wide pot over medium. Cook half the shallots 2 minutes until soft, add half the garlic for 1 minute without browning, then stir in half the flour for 1 minute.
3. **Add the liquid.** Slowly whisk in half the milk, cream and stock until smooth. Whisk in half the Dijon, salt, pepper and nutmeg, and add 4 thyme sprigs and 1 bay leaf.
4. **Simmer the potatoes.** Add half the potatoes and bring to a gentle simmer over medium-low. Cook 12–15 minutes, folding up from the bottom every minute or two so nothing scorches, until the slices bend but hold together. Taste the sauce; it should be boldly seasoned, since the potatoes soak up salt as they bake.
5. **Fill the pan.** Remove the thyme stems and bay leaf and pour everything into the first pan. Press the potatoes level; they should be just covered in sauce. Dot with 1 tbsp butter.
6. **Repeat.** Make the second batch the same way and fill the second pan.
7. **Bake.** Set a sheet pan on the lower rack to catch drips. Bake both pans uncovered 35–45 minutes, swapping racks halfway, until golden brown, bubbling at the edges, and a knife slides through with no resistance.
8. **Rest.** Rest at least 15 minutes so the sauce sets, then scatter the chives over the top.

## Notes

- **Flavor without cheese:** the stock adds savory depth, the Dijon adds sharpness without tasting of mustard, and the thyme, bay and nutmeg give the classic flavor. Season the sauce firmly before baking; it can't be fixed afterward.
- **Enough sauce:** the flour keeps the sauce creamy instead of curdling and stops it from being absorbed away in the oven. If the potatoes aren't covered after simmering, add milk until they are.
- **Why simmer first:** potatoes that go into the oven raw can stay firm in the middle, especially at high altitude (around 5,000 ft), where water boils near 202°F.
- **Vegetarian:** use vegetable stock.
- **To transport:** cover tightly with foil and wrap in towels; the pans stay hot about an hour.
- **To make ahead:** bake, cool and refrigerate. Reheat covered at 350°F for 35–45 minutes, adding a splash of milk around the edges and uncovering for the last 10.
