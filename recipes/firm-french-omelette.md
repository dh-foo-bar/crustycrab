## Ingredients

- 3 large eggs
- ⅛ tsp fine salt
- 1 tbsp unsalted butter, plus a small piece to finish
- 1 tsp finely minced chives, or fines herbes (chives, parsley, tarragon, chervil)
- 1 tbsp herbed soft cheese or crème fraîche, optional

## Steps

1. **Beat and salt early.** Beat the eggs with the salt until completely uniform, with no streaks of white. Rest 10–15 minutes if you can; the salt loosens the egg proteins so they set tender. For extra smoothness, strain through a fine sieve.
2. **Heat the butter gently.** Melt the butter in an 8 in nonstick skillet over medium-low. Add the eggs as the foam starts to subside, before the butter takes on any color.
3. **Scramble small curds.** Stir fast with a silicone spatula while shaking the pan back and forth. Keep going 60–90 seconds, until the curds are mostly set with only a little loose egg left.
4. **Set it firm.** Stop stirring and smooth the eggs into an even layer. Add the filling, if using, down the center along with half the herbs. Leave it 30–60 seconds, until the top is set and just glossy with no wet egg. To speed the top, cover with a lid for 20–30 seconds. If the bottom starts to color, lower the heat.
5. **Roll.** Tilt the pan away from you. Fold the near third toward the center, slide the omelette to the far edge, then tip it onto a warm plate so it rolls over itself and lands seam-side down. Tuck the ends under to shape.
6. **Finish.** Rub the top with the small piece of cold butter for shine, scatter the remaining herbs and serve right away.

## Notes

- **Firm, not brown:** the goal is a fully set interior with a pale, smooth outside. Heat control is the whole technique, so start lower than you think.
- **The pan:** nonstick or well-seasoned carbon steel. Cast iron holds too much heat and grips the eggs.
- **Fillings:** crème fraîche keeps the center softer; a firm herbed cheese holds up better here. Crumbled feta (about 1 oz, patted dry and with less salt in the eggs) also works, added with the herbs.
- **At high altitude (around 5,000 ft):** nothing changes; the eggs never get near boiling.
- **The first one or two may tear.** That's normal.
