## Ingredients

### Chile paste

- 2–3 dried guajillo chiles
- 1–2 dried ancho chiles
- 1 dried árbol chile
- 1 garlic clove
- Pinch of ground cumin
- Hot water, for soaking

### Chili

- 3 lb 90/10 ground beef
- 2 large onions, diced
- 1 bell pepper, diced
- 6 garlic cloves, minced
- 5 cans (15 oz each) kidney or black beans, drained and rinsed
- 2 cans (28 oz each) crushed tomatoes
- 1 can (14.5 oz) diced tomatoes
- 1 can (6 oz) tomato paste
- 2–3 cups beef broth
- 1–2 tbsp chili powder
- 2 tbsp ground cumin
- 1 tbsp dried oregano
- Salt and pepper

## Steps

1. **Toast the chiles.** Toast the dried chiles in a dry skillet until fragrant, about 30 seconds per side.
2. **Soak.** Remove the stems and seeds and soak the chiles in hot water 15–20 minutes, until soft.
3. **Blend the paste.** Blend the chiles with the garlic clove, cumin and a splash of the soaking liquid until smooth. It makes about ½ cup.
4. **Brown the beef.** Brown the beef in a large pot over medium-high, breaking it up as it cooks. Drain the excess fat.
5. **Soften the vegetables.** Add the onions and bell pepper and cook until softened, 5–7 minutes. Add the garlic and cook 1 minute.
6. **Cook the pastes.** Add the chile paste and tomato paste and cook 1–2 minutes, until fragrant and slightly darkened.
7. **Bloom the spices.** Stir in the chili powder, cumin and oregano and cook 30 seconds.
8. **Simmer.** Add the crushed and diced tomatoes, beans and broth and stir well. Bring to a boil, then simmer uncovered 60–90 minutes, stirring occasionally.
9. **Season.** Season with salt and pepper and serve.

## Notes

- **Yield:** about 20 cups; nutrition is per 1-cup serving.
- **Heat:** the single árbol gives a pleasant kick. Add a second to the paste after tasting if you want more; it's easier to add heat than take it away.
- **Why paste:** toasted, rehydrated chiles give a richer, fruitier, earthier chili than chili powder alone, so the powder here is only a supplement.
- **Freezes well** for up to 3 months.
