## Ingredients

### Lamb

- 2 lamb shanks (about 1 lb each)
- 1½ tsp kosher salt, plus black pepper
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 medium yellow onion, diced
- 1 carrot, diced
- 1 celery rib, diced
- 4 garlic cloves, smashed
- 1½ tbsp tomato paste
- 1½ cups dry red wine
- 1½ cups low-sodium beef stock, plus more to top up
- 2 sprigs rosemary, 4 sprigs thyme, 1 bay leaf
- 1 tbsp cold butter, to finish the sauce

### Pickled red onions

- 1 medium red onion, sliced ⅛ in thick
- ½ cup red wine vinegar
- ½ cup warm water
- 1 tbsp sugar, 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black peppercorns, 1 bay leaf
- 2 tbsp chopped flat-leaf parsley, at serving

### Mashed potatoes

- 1¼ lb Yukon gold potatoes, peeled, in 1½ in chunks
- Kosher salt, for the water
- 3 tbsp unsalted butter
- ½ cup whole milk or cream, warmed

## Steps

1. **Pickle the onions first.** Dissolve the sugar and salt in the warm water, then stir in the vinegar. Put the peppercorns and bay leaf in a glass jar, pack in the onions, and pour the brine over to cover. Rest at least 1 hour, in glass or ceramic rather than metal.
2. **Season.** Set a rack in the lower-middle of the oven and heat it to 285°F. Pat the shanks dry and season them all over with the salt and pepper.
3. **Sear.** Heat the oil in an enameled Dutch oven over medium-high until shimmering. Brown the shanks well on all sides, 8–10 minutes total. Set them aside.
4. **Cook the aromatics.** Pour off all but about 1 tbsp fat. Over medium, cook the onion, carrot and celery until soft, 5–6 minutes. Add the garlic and tomato paste and cook 2 minutes, until the paste darkens.
5. **Deglaze.** Add the wine, scrape up the browned bits, and simmer until reduced by about half, about 5 minutes. Add the stock, rosemary, thyme and bay leaf.
6. **Assemble.** Nestle in the shanks. The liquid should reach two-thirds of the way up the shanks; add stock or water to get there. Bring to a bare simmer on the stove.
7. **Cover.** Cut a parchment circle the diameter of the pot with a small vent in the center and press it onto the surface of the liquid. Put the lid on. If you have a probe thermometer, sink the tip into the liquid beside a shank, not touching bone or pot, with the wire under the lid's edge. Move the pot to the oven.
8. **Braise.** The target is liquid at 190–200°F. If it holds at a rolling boil, drop the oven 15–25°F. At 90 minutes, turn the shanks and check the level; top up with hot stock if it is below halfway.
9. **Brown.** At about 2½ hours, remove the lid and parchment. Cook uncovered 30–45 minutes, turning once. The shanks are done when a fork slides in with no resistance and the meat pulls from the bone; if not, keep going and check every 20 minutes. Total time is 3–3½ hours.
10. **Make the sauce.** Move the shanks to a plate and tent with foil. Strain the liquid, pressing on the solids. Let it settle 5 minutes and skim the fat. Reduce over medium until it coats a spoon, about ¾ cup. Off the heat, swirl in the butter and season. Return the shanks and baste.
11. **Boil the potatoes.** About 40 minutes before the lamb is done, cover the potatoes by 1 in with cold, well-salted water. Bring to a boil, then simmer until a knife slides in with no resistance, 20–25 minutes.
12. **Mash.** Drain, then return the potatoes to the pot over low heat for 1–2 minutes to steam off moisture. Mash, stir in the butter first, then the warm milk a splash at a time. Season.
13. **Serve.** Spoon the mash onto plates, set a shank on top and spoon over the sauce. Drain a portion of onions, toss with the parsley, and pile alongside.

## Notes

- **At high altitude (around 5,000 ft):** the braising liquid can't get past about 203°F, so extra oven heat only boils it away. That is why the oven sits at 285°F: you want a bare simmer, an occasional lazy bubble. Braises also run about 25% longer, so go by fork-tenderness, not the clock. The sauce reduces quickly, so watch it.
- **No probe:** check at 90 minutes. A steady boil means turn the oven down 15–25°F. Opening the lid for a quick look is fine.
- **Make ahead:** the shanks improve overnight in the sauce, and the chilled fat lifts right off. The onions keep 1–2 weeks refrigerated; add parsley only to what you're serving.
- **Gremolata touch:** a little lemon zest tossed with the parsley and onions brings it close to the classic topping for braised shanks.
