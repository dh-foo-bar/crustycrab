## Ingredients

### Birria braise

- About 3 lb boneless chuck roast, cut into 4 pieces (or 4 lb bone-in short ribs)
- 2 tsp kosher salt
- 1 tsp freshly ground black pepper
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 5 dried guajillo chiles, stemmed and seeded
- 3 dried ancho chiles, stemmed and seeded
- 2 dried chiles de árbol, stemmed
- 4 Roma tomatoes, halved
- ½ white onion, quartered
- 6 garlic cloves, peeled
- 2 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp Mexican oregano
- ½ tsp ground cinnamon
- 4 whole cloves
- 2 bay leaves
- 2 tbsp apple cider vinegar
- 4 cups low-sodium beef broth

### Pappardelle

- 400 g 00 or all-purpose flour
- 4 large eggs plus 2 yolks
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- ½ tsp salt
- Semolina or flour, for dusting

### To finish

- ½ cup crumbled cotija
- ½ cup chopped cilantro
- ½ cup finely diced white onion
- 2 limes, in wedges

## Steps

1. **Season.** Pat the beef dry and season it with the salt only; the pepper goes in after searing so it doesn't scorch.
2. **Toast and soak the chiles.** Toast the guajillo, ancho and árbol chiles in a dry skillet over medium heat 30–60 seconds per side, until fragrant and pliable, without burning. Cover with hot water and soak 20 minutes.
3. **Char the aromatics.** Char the tomatoes, onion and garlic over a gas flame or in a very hot dry cast iron skillet until blackened in spots, 6–8 minutes.
4. **Blend the adobo.** Drain the chiles, reserving 1 cup of the soaking liquid. Blend the chiles, charred vegetables, cumin, oregano, cinnamon, cloves, vinegar and reserved liquid on high until completely smooth, 2–3 minutes. Strain through a fine-mesh sieve.
5. **Sear.** Heat the oil in a Dutch oven (or a pressure cooker on high sauté, fully preheated) over medium-high until shimmering. Sear the beef in batches, 3–4 minutes per side, until deeply browned. Set aside.
6. **Build the braise.** Pour off all but 1 tbsp fat. Add the adobo and fry it, stirring, until it darkens and thickens, 4–5 minutes. Add the broth, bay leaves and pepper, scraping up the browned bits, and nestle the beef back in with its juices; the liquid should come about two-thirds of the way up.
7. **Braise.** Oven: cover and braise at 325°F for 3–3½ hours, until the meat shreds easily, checking at 2½ hours and adding ½ cup water if the liquid has reduced too far. Pressure cooker: make sure nothing is stuck to the bottom, then cook on high pressure 50 minutes with a 15-minute natural release.
8. **Make the dough.** While the beef braises, mound the flour, make a well, and add the eggs, yolks, olive oil and salt. Beat with a fork, gradually pulling in flour from the walls, then knead 8–10 minutes until smooth and elastic. Wrap and rest at least 30 minutes.
9. **Roll and cut.** Divide the dough into 4 pieces, keeping the rest wrapped. Roll each through a pasta roller from the widest setting down to 5 or 6, leaving it slightly thicker than tagliatelle. Flour the sheets, cut them into 12 in lengths, and cut by hand into 1 in ribbons. Toss with semolina and set in loose nests under a towel.
10. **Finish the sauce.** Lift out the beef, discard any bones and the bay leaves, and shred the meat with two forks. Skim the fat from the liquid and taste for salt. If it's thin, simmer uncovered 10–15 minutes, until loose but coating, like a thinned ragù. Return the meat.
11. **Cook the pasta.** Boil the pappardelle in well-salted water 2–3 minutes. Reserve 1 cup pasta water before draining.
12. **Toss.** Add the pasta to the sauce and toss over medium heat 1–2 minutes, adding pasta water as needed, until glossy and coated rather than swimming.
13. **Serve.** Top with cotija, cilantro and onion, with lime wedges for squeezing.

## Notes

- **Cut size:** keep the beef in big pieces, 2½–3 in or simply quartered; small cubes dry out before they turn tender.
- **Pressure cooker:** the thick, tomato-heavy adobo can trigger a burn warning, so deglaze thoroughly before sealing. The oven braise gives a little more depth; the pressure cooker saves about 2 hours.
- **Heat:** 2 árbol chiles give gentle warmth. Use 4–5 for real heat or leave them out.
- **Chile swap:** dried New Mexico chiles can stand in for missing anchos at about 2 for 1.
- **Dried pasta:** mafaldine or dried pappardelle work too; reserve more pasta water, since dried pasta is thirstier.
- **At high altitude (around 5,000 ft):** the oven braise runs longer than sea-level recipes, so budget the extra time. The pasta dough may need 1–2 tsp extra water.
