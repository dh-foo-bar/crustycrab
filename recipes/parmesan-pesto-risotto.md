## Ingredients

- 8 cups low-sodium vegetable or chicken stock, plus hot water as needed
- 4 tbsp unsalted butter, divided
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 2 shallots, finely diced
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 cups arborio rice
- ¾ cup dry white wine
- 1½ cups finely grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- ⅓ cup basil pesto, optional, for half the batch

## Steps

1. **Heat the stock.** Bring the stock to a bare simmer and keep it warm over low heat, with a kettle of hot water standing by in case you run short.
2. **Soften the aromatics.** In a wide, heavy pan, melt 2 tbsp butter with the olive oil over medium. Cook the shallots until translucent, then add the garlic and cook 1 minute, until fragrant.
3. **Toast the rice.** Add the rice and stir to coat. Toast about 2 minutes, until the edges turn translucent and it smells faintly nutty.
4. **Deglaze.** Pour in the wine and stir constantly until it's almost fully absorbed.
5. **Add the stock gradually.** Add the warm stock one ladle (about ½ cup) at a time, stirring often and letting each addition mostly absorb before the next. Keep it at a gentle simmer.
6. **Cook to al dente.** Keep ladling and stirring for 20–25 minutes, tasting as you go, until creamy with a slight bite at the center. Add hot water if the stock runs out.
7. **Finish.** Off the heat, stir in the remaining 2 tbsp butter, the Parmesan, salt and pepper and beat vigorously for 30 seconds. Loosen with a splash of stock so it flows slightly rather than clumping.
8. **Split and add pesto.** For two flavors, plate half now. Stir the pesto into the other half over low heat until streaky, loosening with stock as needed.

## Notes

- **Reheating:** risotto firms up as it cools. Warm it over medium-low with a splash of stock or water, stirring until creamy again. The pesto half is best eaten first, since basil fades faster.
- **At high altitude (around 5,000 ft):** liquid evaporates and absorbs faster, so you'll use more stock, and the rice takes a few extra minutes; plan on 28–32 minutes and start tasting at 25.
